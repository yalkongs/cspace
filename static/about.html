About

How the book was made, what it rests on, and the license under which it is shared.

This essay is published anonymously under the wordmark The Book of Color. The author has stepped back so that the work — and the colours it discusses — can be read without the distraction of personality. If you wish to cite it, the publisher of record is "The Book of Color" itself.

How it was made

The essay is a single HTML file. There is no framework. There is no build pipeline beyond a small Python translator that produces ten language versions from one source. There is no analytics. There are no accounts. There is no newsletter that asks for your email.

All of the interactive figures — the prism, the spectrum slider, the cone-response graph, the metamerism analyzer, the OKLCH solid comparator, the Land two-colour projection, the Hering cancellation experiment, the harmony explorer, the gamut viewfinder, and the rest — are written in vanilla JavaScript with HTML5 Canvas and SVG. No Three.js, no D3, no React. The reasoning is simple: the essay should outlast its tooling, and the reader's machine should pay no rent.

Where a figure departs from the received physical values (most notably the deliberately exaggerated dispersion in §1, Newton's Prism, where the Cauchy coefficient is set to roughly four times that of true crown glass so the fan is visible at the canvas size), the deviation is acknowledged in the figure's caption.

Method, in one sentence

Each chapter is written, then a figure is built that lets the reader move what the text describes; then the figure is checked against the source it claims to follow.

Principal sources

Every chapter rests on primary literature or its accepted modern synthesis. The principal references are:

  • Newton, I. Opticks: or, A Treatise of the Reflexions, Refractions, Inflexions and Colours of Light. London, 1704.
  • Goethe, J. W. von. Zur Farbenlehre. Tübingen, 1810.
  • Young, T. "The Bakerian Lecture: On the Theory of Light and Colours." Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society 92 (1802): 12–48.
  • Helmholtz, H. von. Handbuch der physiologischen Optik. Leipzig, 1867.
  • Maxwell, J. C. "On the Theory of Compound Colours, and the Relations of the Colours of the Spectrum." Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society 150 (1860): 57–84.
  • Chevreul, M.-E. De la loi du contraste simultané des couleurs. Paris, 1839.
  • Hering, E. Zur Lehre vom Lichtsinne. Vienna, 1878.
  • Helmholtz, H. von & Kohlrausch, F. Effect on perceived brightness from chromatic stimuli, in Annalen der Physik, 1907.
  • Munsell, A. H. A Color Notation. Boston, 1905.
  • Kubelka, P. & Munk, F. "Ein Beitrag zur Optik der Farbanstriche." Zeitschrift für technische Physik 12 (1931): 593–601.
  • CIE. CIE 1931 Standard Colorimetric Observer. Commission internationale de l'éclairage, 1931 (and its subsequent revisions).
  • Itten, J. Kunst der Farbe. Ravensburg, 1961.
  • Albers, J. Interaction of Color. Yale University Press, 1963.
  • Land, E. H. "Experiments in Color Vision." Scientific American 200, no. 5 (May 1959): 84–99.
  • Land, E. H. & McCann, J. J. "Lightness and Retinex Theory." Journal of the Optical Society of America 61 (1971): 1–11.
  • CIE. Recommendations on Uniform Color Spaces — Color-Difference Equations, Psychometric Color Terms. Supplement No. 2 to CIE Publication No. 15 (E.-1.3.1) 1971/(TC-1.3.) 1978.
  • Ottosson, B. "A perceptual color space for image processing." bottosson.github.io, 2020.
  • Berlin, B. & Kay, P. Basic Color Terms: Their Universality and Evolution. University of California Press, 1969.
  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2. W3C Recommendation, 2023.
  • APCA-W3 (Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm). Myndex Research, 2019–.

The book is a synthesis, not original research. Where it adds anything of its own, that addition is in the figures — the live, manipulable demonstrations that let a reader feel what the text only describes.

Languages

The essay is available in English, 한국어, 日本語, Español, 中文, Français, Deutsch, Português, Italiano, and Tiếng Việt, with a partial Bahasa Indonesia edition. Some passages retain English fallback text; translations are a work in progress.

Each translation is a first draft by a large language model, reviewed for terminology fidelity against a per-language locked glossary (Newton, Goethe, Munsell, Itten, Albers, Ottosson, etc. all spelled per the host language's scholarly convention). The book welcomes corrections.

Contact & corrections

The book deliberately collects no email addresses and runs no feedback form — both would compromise the analytics-free promise. If you have a correction, a fact to add, or a translation suggestion, the simplest channel is to post it publicly anywhere on the web; the project monitors mentions through search-engine alerts on the exact phrase "The Book of Color".
